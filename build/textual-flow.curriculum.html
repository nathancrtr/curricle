Textual criticism & computational philology

Learn New Testament textual criticism and computational philology by building a simulation & sensitivity laboratory for the CBGM — with Koine Greek alongside, start to finish.

Learn New Testament textual criticism and computational philology by building textual-flow — a simulation and sensitivity laboratory for the Coherence-Based Genealogical Method (CBGM) — through to three research deliverables: a simulation study with known ground truth, a sensitivity analysis of a published critical text, and an LLM local-stemma experiment. For a senior software engineer at ~3–5 hrs/week; roughly 18–24 months, in phases that each stand alone.

How this curriculum works

The research program is the spine. This is a research-driven course: the artifact you're building is a research question and its answer, not just an app. The question (versioned in the repo's REVIEW.md and exploration/convergence.md): how robust is the CBGM — and the New Testament text it now decides — to the human judgments and structural assumptions inside it? Every unit either builds a piece of the apparatus that answers it or builds the background you need to answer it credibly. Where prior work exists (open-cbgm, the artificial-tradition literature), you read it as worked examples; where it doesn't (everything this program contributes), you build it.

Two tracks, interleaved. The program track (~2–3 hrs/wk) builds the laboratory. The Greek track (~1.5–2 hrs/wk, steady) runs continuously from week 0 — languages reward little-and-often far more than bingeing, so treat it as a near-daily 15–25 minute habit rather than a weekly block. Each phase states its Greek goal; the program track is scheduled assuming Greek is happening alongside. The two converge deliberately: the Johannine epistles are simultaneously the simplest Greek in the NT and the ECM data your laboratory runs on. By Phase 5 you will be reading, in Greek, the very variation units your perturbation harness is flipping.

Each unit has: Build (the implementation step) · Read (precise sources — see learning-resources.md for every citation) · Concepts · Exercise · Milestone, and sometimes a Key Insight or an interactive piece in interactive/.

Pacing. One unit ≈ one week at the stated hours, but the variance is real: Unit 3 (genealogical coherence) and Unit 12 (the experiment grid) run long; Units 5 and 14 run short. Phases end with checkpoints; if a checkpoint feels shaky, camp there — the dependency map at the end shows what is load-bearing.

Tests are your grader. From Unit 2 onward you maintain a test suite. The house style (inherited from rhyme-schemer): structural property tests and ordering assertions you trust over exact numbers — plus, unique to this project, cross-validation against open-cbgm: wherever you reimplement a CBGM computation, a test asserts your numbers match Joey McCollum's implementation on the same input. When they disagree, one of you is wrong, and finding out which is always worth the hour.

Reception discipline is part of the method. This field has scar tissue (Morton, Radday — see exploration/field-map.md §4). Every written deliverable follows the rules learned there: calibrated instrument with stated uncertainty, validated on ground truth the guild accepts, never "the computer settles X." The write-up units bake this in.
